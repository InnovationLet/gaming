The Epic Games Store takes 12% of every sale, while Steam, the PlayStation Store, and the App Store take 30% by default. On a $60 game, that gap is $10.80 per copy, and across a million copies it's money that decides where a game launches.

## How the standard split works

The 70/30 split has been the default since the days of physical retail, where the store's cut covered shelf space, stock, and shipping. Digital storefronts kept the number even after those costs mostly went away. Steam drops to 80/20 once a title passes $10 million in revenue, and mobile stores match 85/15 on a developer's first $1 million each year.

Deals vary a lot above that base. A console maker might pay for a timed exclusive, a service like Game Pass can license a game outright for a flat fee, and a publisher taking on marketing often negotiates a larger share. The headline rate is only the starting point.

## Where the money sends games

A high split is one reason free-to-play games cluster on PC and mobile instead of consoles. If a game earns most of its revenue from microtransactions spread over years, an extra 15 points of margin compounds into a huge amount. It also explains why some premium games skip an exclusivity check and stay everywhere at once.

Timed exclusives work the other way. A studio that takes an advance from Epic can cover development costs before a single unit sells, which matters enormously when the runway is short. The trade is real, though. Players on other stores wait, and goodwill is hard to price.

## What gets made under these terms

Splits quietly shape design. A game built on a store with a 30% cut has to earn back nearly a third more revenue to match the same profit, which pushes teams toward DLC, season passes, and storefronts where they keep more. Studios without a safety net often pick the platform that pays fastest, not the one with the biggest audience.

**Takeaway:** Before choosing where to launch, model your actual margin at each store's rate, including the tier where the rate changes. Then check what the platform offers for its cut (featuring slots, wish-list mechanics, advance payments) and decide if that's worth the difference.
